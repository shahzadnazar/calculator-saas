Protein gets a lot of attention, and for good reason: it builds and repairs muscle, keeps you full, and supports nearly every process in the body. But how much you actually need depends on your weight and how active you are. This guide gives you a target — and a plan to hit it.

## Your number depends on your goal

Protein needs scale with body weight, expressed in grams per kilogram:

- **0.8 g/kg** — the baseline for sedentary adults (the official minimum to avoid deficiency).
- **1.2–1.4 g/kg** — active people and endurance athletes.
- **1.6–1.8 g/kg** — those strength training or trying to build muscle.
- **Up to 2.2 g/kg** — preserving muscle while dieting in a calorie deficit.

An 80 kg person aiming to build muscle would target roughly `80 × 1.8 = 144 g` per day.

## Why protein matters more when you're losing weight

When you cut calories, your body can burn muscle along with fat. Eating plenty of protein — and doing some resistance training — tells it to hold on to muscle instead. That keeps your metabolism higher and your results better. It's also the most **filling** macronutrient, which makes a diet easier to stick to.

## Hitting your target with food

Protein is easier to get than people think:

- Chicken breast (~31 g per 100 g), fish, and lean beef.
- Eggs (~6 g each), Greek yogurt, cottage cheese and milk.
- Lentils, beans, tofu, tempeh and edamame for plant-based sources.

Spreading protein across your meals — rather than loading it all at dinner — helps your body use it effectively.

## Fit it into your calories

Protein is part of a bigger picture. Once you know your protein target, check your overall energy needs with the [calorie calculator](/health/calorie-calculator) so your protein goal fits inside a sensible daily total. Get your number from the [protein calculator](/health/protein-calculator).

*This is general guidance, not medical advice. People with kidney conditions should speak with a doctor before increasing protein.*
