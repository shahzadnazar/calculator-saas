Fractions describe parts of a whole, and once you know the four rules for combining them, they are no harder than working with whole numbers. This guide covers each operation, why it works, and how to tidy up your answer.

## Adding and subtracting: find a common denominator

You can only add or subtract fractions that share the same denominator (bottom number). To add `1/2 + 1/3`, convert both to sixths: `3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6`. The shortcut is to multiply the denominators for the new denominator, and cross-multiply for the numerators.

## Multiplying: straight across

Multiplication is the easy one — multiply the numerators together and the denominators together. `2/3 × 3/4 = 6/12`, which simplifies to `1/2`. No common denominator needed.

## Dividing: flip and multiply

To divide by a fraction, flip the second one (its **reciprocal**) and multiply. `1/2 ÷ 1/4` becomes `1/2 × 4/1 = 4/2 = 2`. It feels like a trick, but it follows directly from what division means.

## Simplifying and mixed numbers

Always reduce your answer to **lowest terms** by dividing the top and bottom by their greatest common divisor: `6/12` becomes `1/2`. If the numerator is larger than the denominator (an *improper* fraction), you can write it as a **mixed number** — `7/2` is `3 1/2`.

## Converting to a decimal

Divide the numerator by the denominator: `5/8 = 0.625`. Decimals are handy for comparisons and for entering values into other calculators.

## Where fractions show up

Cooking (half of ¾ cup), construction (⅝-inch bolt), and probability all rely on fractions. The [fraction calculator](/math/fraction-calculator) shows the simplified fraction, the mixed number and the decimal at once, so you can check your work in whichever form you need.
