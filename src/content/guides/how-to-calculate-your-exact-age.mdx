Your age in years is easy. Your **exact** age — years, months and days — is surprisingly fiddly, because months have different lengths and leap years add a day. This guide explains the pitfalls and how to get an accurate answer for any two dates.

## Why simple subtraction fails

You might think you can just subtract the birth date from today. But what is "one month" — 28 days? 30? 31? And how many February 29ths fell in between? Doing it by hand, it's easy to be off by a day or two. The reliable method counts complete years first, then remaining whole months, then leftover days — borrowing correctly from the previous month when needed.

## Leap years matter

Between 2000 and 2020 there were **five** leap days (2000, 2004, 2008, 2012, 2016), so that span is 7,305 days, not `20 × 365 = 7,300`. Any accurate age or duration calculation has to count those extra days — which is exactly what the calculator above does.

## When exact age is more than trivia

- **Legal thresholds** — driving, voting, retirement benefits often hinge on an exact date.
- **Medical dosing and milestones** — especially for infants, measured in weeks and months.
- **Countdowns** — days until a birthday or anniversary.

## Age at any date

You aren't limited to "today." Set the second date to any point in the past or future to find how old someone was — or will be — on that day. Useful for planning events, checking eligibility, or settling a friendly argument.
