Three acronyms show up again and again in health and fitness: **BMI**, **BMR** and **TDEE**. They sound technical, but each answers a simple question, and together they give you a clear starting point for managing your weight. This guide explains what each one means and how to use them.

## BMI: are you in a healthy weight range?

**Body Mass Index (BMI)** compares your weight to your height. It is a quick screening tool that sorts adults into underweight, healthy, overweight and obese ranges. It does not measure body fat directly — a very muscular person can register as "overweight" — but for most people it is a useful first check.

The World Health Organization classifies a BMI of **18.5–24.9** as a healthy weight. If yours falls outside that range, it is a signal to look closer, not a diagnosis. For a better picture of body composition, pair BMI with the [body fat calculator](/health/body-fat-calculator).

## BMR: your body at rest

**Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)** is the number of calories your body burns at complete rest, just to keep you alive — breathing, circulating blood, maintaining temperature. It typically accounts for **60–70%** of the calories you burn each day, and it depends on your sex, age, height and weight. You can calculate it directly with the [BMR calculator](/health/bmr-calculator).

## TDEE: your real daily calorie needs

Your **Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)** is your BMR multiplied by an activity factor — how much you move and exercise. This is the number that actually matters for managing weight:

- Eat around your TDEE to **maintain** your weight.
- Eat below it to **lose** weight (a deficit of ~500 calories a day is roughly 0.5 kg per week).
- Eat above it to **gain** weight.

## Putting it together

Here is a simple, sustainable way to use all three:

1. **Check your BMI** to see whether a weight change makes sense.
2. **Find your TDEE** with the calorie calculator to learn your maintenance calories.
3. **Set a modest deficit or surplus** — around 250–500 calories — for gradual, lasting change.
4. **Support it with protein** using the [protein calculator](/health/protein-calculator), especially if you are exercising.

Small, consistent adjustments beat drastic ones. Recalculate every few weeks as your weight changes, since your BMR and TDEE shift with it.
