The internet is drowning in weight-loss advice, most of it contradictory and some of it harmful. Underneath the noise, though, the science is refreshingly simple. This guide connects the key measurements — BMI, body fat, BMR and calories — into one clear, sustainable framework.

## Step 1: Know where you're starting

Two numbers give you a baseline.

**Body Mass Index (BMI)** is a quick screen comparing weight to height. It doesn't measure fat directly, but for most people it's a useful first check.

<BmiCalculator />

**Body fat percentage** is the better measure of composition, because it separates fat from muscle. A muscular person can have a "high" BMI yet low body fat. Use the [body fat calculator](/health/body-fat-calculator) alongside BMI, and the [ideal weight calculator](/health/ideal-weight-calculator) for a realistic target range for your height. The lesson: don't fixate on a single number on the scale — look at the whole picture.

## Step 2: Understand energy balance

Weight change comes down to one principle: **energy in vs. energy out**. Eat more energy (calories) than you burn and you gain weight; eat less and you lose it. Everything else — which diet, which foods, which schedule — is just a strategy for managing that balance.

Two numbers define your "energy out":

- **BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate)** — the calories your body burns at complete rest. Find yours with the [BMR calculator](/health/bmr-calculator).
- **TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure)** — your BMR multiplied by an activity factor. This is your real daily calorie need.

## Step 3: Set a sensible target

Once you know your maintenance calories (TDEE):

- To **lose** weight, eat a modest deficit — about **250–500 calories** below maintenance for roughly 0.25–0.5 kg (0.5–1 lb) per week.
- To **maintain**, eat around your TDEE.
- To **gain** (muscle), eat a small surplus paired with resistance training.

Avoid extreme deficits. Crash diets cost you muscle, slow your metabolism, and rarely last. Slow and steady genuinely wins here.

## Step 4: Protect your muscle with protein

When you're in a calorie deficit, your body can burn muscle along with fat. Two things prevent that: **enough protein** and **resistance training**. Keeping muscle matters because muscle burns more energy at rest — so it keeps your BMR higher and makes maintenance easier. Use the [protein calculator](/health/protein-calculator) to set a daily target (often 1.6–2.2 g/kg when losing fat).

## Step 5: Make it sustainable

The best plan is the one you can stick to. A few principles that survive contact with real life:

- **Prioritise protein and fibre** — they keep you full on fewer calories.
- **Don't ban foods** — room for treats prevents the all-or-nothing cycle.
- **Track trends, not days** — weight fluctuates daily with water and food; watch the weekly average.
- **Recalculate periodically** — as your weight changes, so do your BMR and TDEE.
- **Build habits, not deadlines** — walking, sleep and consistency beat any 30-day challenge.

## Putting it all together

1. Check your **[BMI](/health/bmi-calculator)** and **[body fat](/health/body-fat-calculator)** for a baseline.
2. Find your **[TDEE](/health/calorie-calculator)** to learn your maintenance calories.
3. Set a **modest deficit or surplus** toward your goal.
4. Hit a **[protein](/health/protein-calculator)** target and train to keep muscle.
5. Adjust every few weeks and stay consistent.

No fads, no misery — just the numbers, used well.

*This guide is general educational information, not medical advice. Talk to a qualified healthcare professional before starting a weight-loss or exercise program, especially if you have any health conditions.*
