The bathroom scale can't tell the difference between muscle and fat — but your health depends on it. **Body fat percentage**, the share of your weight that is fat, is a far better indicator of fitness than weight alone. This guide covers what's healthy, how to measure it without a lab, and how to change it.

## What counts as healthy

Body fat needs differ by sex, because women carry more essential fat. As rough guides:

- **Men:** 6–13% is athletic, 14–17% is fit, 18–24% is average, and 25%+ is classed as obese.
- **Women:** 14–20% is athletic, 21–24% is fit, 25–31% is average, and 32%+ is classed as obese.

A little fat is **essential** — around 2–5% for men and 10–13% for women — for hormones, insulation and organ protection. Lower is not better.

## Measuring at home

The calculator above uses the **U.S. Navy circumference method**: with just a tape measure and your height, it estimates body fat from neck and waist measurements (plus hips for women). It's not as precise as a DEXA scan, but it's free, repeatable, and good for tracking trends over time. Measure first thing in the morning and take each measurement twice.

## Body fat vs. BMI

[BMI](/health/bmi-calculator) is quicker but blind to composition — a muscular athlete can register as "overweight." Body fat percentage sees through that. Use them together: BMI for a fast screen, body fat for the real picture.

## Lowering body fat sustainably

There's no shortcut, but the fundamentals work:

- **A modest calorie deficit** — around 250–500 calories a day — for gradual loss.
- **Enough protein** to preserve muscle while you lose fat.
- **Resistance training** to keep (or build) lean mass, which keeps your metabolism higher.

Track your body fat every few weeks rather than daily — it moves slowly, and the trend matters more than any single reading. Start with the [body fat calculator](/health/body-fat-calculator).
