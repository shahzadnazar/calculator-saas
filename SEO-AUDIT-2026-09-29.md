## 2. Real gaps, in priority order

### 2.1 `dateModified` is stale on the three pages just expanded — **FIXED**

| Page | words now | `dateModified` says |
|---|---|---|
| `/finance/credit-card-payoff-calculator` | 3,029 | 2026-08-27 |
| `/everyday/square-footage-calculator` | 3,407 | 2026-08-29 |
| `/everyday/concrete-calculator` | 3,518 | **2026-07-25** |

About 2,500 words were added across these three pages in the last three commits and both
the JSON-LD `dateModified` and the visible "reviewed for accuracy on" line still carried the
pre-rewrite date — concrete's was two months stale. Google was being told nothing changed on
the exact pages where the most had.

All three `reviewedDate` values are now `2026-09-29`, which the page prints, emits as
`dateModified` and — since 2.2 — publishes as its sitemap `lastmod`.

### 2.2 Sitemap carries no `lastmod` — **FIXED**

All 111 entries carry `<changefreq>weekly</changefreq>` and `<priority>0.7</priority>` —
two hints Google has said publicly it ignores — and omit `<lastmod>`, the one it acts on.
For a domain with no index history, `lastmod` is the main lever for getting a recrawl
after an edit.

`scripts/lastmod.mjs` now supplies one per URL through the integration's `serialize` hook,
read from the only two places the site records a date: a calculator's `reviewedDate` and a
guide's `updatedDate ?? publishDate`. That covers **72 of 111 URLs** — all 50 calculators
and all 22 guides. The 39 hubs, reference tables and legal pages carry no date of their own
and get no `lastmod`, because Google's position is that an inaccurate one is worse than a
missing one, and the spec makes the element optional per URL. `changefreq` and `priority`
are kept: they cost nothing and other crawlers still read them.

### 2.3 `assert:indexable` does not protect the description work — **FIXED**

The gate's thresholds are `DESCRIPTION_MIN = 70`, `DESCRIPTION_MAX = 160`,
`TITLE_MAX = 70`, and it checks title/description **only on the homepage**. A regression to
a 75-character description on any calculator would pass CI silently. It currently lets
through:

- **12 pages with descriptions under 120 chars** — `/terms` (46), `/disclaimer` (92),
  `/contact` (104), `/reference/target-heart-rate-table` (104), `/finance` (108),
  `/health` (110), `/math` (110), `/everyday` (111),
  `/reference/salary-conversion-table` (111),
  `/reference/inflation-purchasing-power-table` (112), `/privacy` (113),
  `/reference/monthly-investment-table` (113)
- **10 pages over 160** — `/tasks` (187), `/tasks/health-fitness` (180),
  `/tasks/math-school-tools` (180), `/reference` (166), `/embed` (166),
  `/guides/understanding-compound-interest` (166), and four at 162–163
- **11 titles over 60 chars** — worst: `/reference/monthly-investment-table` (82),
  `/guides/how-vat-works` (75), `/reference/mortgage-payment-table` (74)

The 50 calculators are all inside the band. It was the hubs, reference tables and legal
pages that were never swept — and the gate had no opinion about them.

The gate is now 120–160 / title ≤ 60 **on every public page**, counted on the text Google
renders rather than the source bytes (a single `&` is five characters of HTML and one in
the SERP — that distinction alone accounted for three false failures). It also checks that
every `lastmod` matches the `dateModified` the page itself publishes, so the two signals
cannot drift apart. All 22 pages were rewritten into the band; all 111 now pass.

Five negative controls were run against a mutated bundle — short description, long
description, long title, no `lastmod` at all, and a `lastmod` contradicting its page — and
each one fails the gate.

| `/finance/credit-card-payoff-calculator` | **4** | 22,200 | **28** |

The two pages with the most internal support were the two least winnable. The four best
volume-to-difficulty targets on the site — credit-card-payoff, square-footage, concrete,
income-tax — sat at 4, 14, 13 and 5.

**Cause.** `getRelatedCalculators` was `[...sameCategory, ...others].slice(0, limit)` with
both halves in *registry order* — the order entries happen to be authored in. Two things
followed. Every cross-category slot on the site went to the top of the finance list, which
is why Mortgage and Loan appeared on BMI, fraction and password pages. And finance has 20
entries against a 12-slot rail, so the last eight finance calculators in authoring order
were never linked from a sibling at all — credit-card-payoff among them. Nothing in the
code said "give the first two finance entries every spare slot"; it fell out of `slice()`.

**Fix.** `src/lib/related.ts` ranks by actual relatedness, using signals the site already
maintains: the task group (which `tasks.test.ts` guarantees partitions all 50), topic
cluster membership, the category, and keyword overlap as a tie-break. Inclusion needs a
*structural* tie — keyword overlap alone paired BMI with Inflation off one incidental word
— so cross-category relatedness is exactly what the curated clusters say it is. A tool that
scores zero is left out rather than padding the list, because a rail headed "Related
calculators" that lists a mortgage under a BMI result is both a worse answer and a link the
page should not be spending.

### 2.5 Six topic pages are effectively orphaned — **FIXED**

`/topics/body-and-diet-calculators`, `/topics/savings-investment-calculators`,
`/topics/time-and-date-calculators`, `/topics/geometry-calculators`,
`/topics/loan-calculators` — **2 inbound pages each**, one from `/topics` and one from a
task hub. That is not a pillar, it is an orphan.

The cause was a half-built feature. `getClustersForCalculator` exists in `clusters.ts` with
the docstring "for the reverse 'Part of' links on calculator pages" — and was never called.
The pillar–cluster model only ever ran downward: each hub linked to its members and nothing
linked back. `CalculatorLayout` now closes the loop with one line under the related grid
("Choosing between them? *Loan & Mortgage Calculators* explains when to reach for each
one."), which takes the hubs to **6–10 inbound each**.

### 2.6 602 content H2s have no `id` — no anchors, no TOC

Across the 50 calculator pages, 602 content headings carry no `id`. (The 235 that do are
the in-tool shape/mode headings.) On pages running 1,350–3,500 words that means:

- no jump-to-section sitelinks in the SERP,
- no linkable anchors for the internal linking in 2.4,
- nothing to skim by, which undercuts the point of the content expansion.

### 2.7 One title collision

`/health` — "Health & Fitness Calculators & Tools" vs
`/tasks/health-fitness` — "Health & Fitness Calculators".
Same primary phrase on two hubs, both indexable. Pick one to own it.

### 2.8 32 pages skip H1 → H3

Systematic, not sporadic: the result panel's H3 renders before the first content H2, so
the document outline jumps a level. Not a ranking factor, but it is what a Semrush or
Screaming Frog crawl reports as a structure error, and it does affect screen-reader
navigation.

### 2.9 No forced HTTP → HTTPS redirect in our config

`.htaccess` forces HTTPS only on the `www` branch. `http://bestcalculate.com/x` is not
rewritten by anything we ship. Hostinger may be doing it at the panel level — **worth
checking on the live site**, since I can't. If not, it is one `RewriteCond`.

### 2.10 No HSTS, no Permissions-Policy

`X-Content-Type-Options` and `Referrer-Policy` are set. `Strict-Transport-Security` and
`Permissions-Policy` are not. Note that `X-Frame-Options` must **stay** unset — the embed
product depends on being framed.

### 2.11 Organization JSON-LD still has no `sameAs`

Unchanged open item. This is why Google's AI Overview conflates the brand with an
unrelated iOS app. It needs real profile URLs from you — I can't invent them.

## 3. Google AdSense readiness

**The architecture is right and nothing needs restructuring.** Verified: `enabled: false`
globally and on all eight placements; `MonetizationRegion` renders as a sibling of
`ResultShell`, never inside it; the post-result region is revealed only for a fresh valid
result, scoped to `[data-calculator-workspace]`; `.mon-*` CSS is emitted inline only when
active, so zero monetization bytes ship today; `assert:mon-off` guards it in CI.

Three gaps stand between that and serving ads:

1. **No `ads.txt`.** AdSense warns about this from day one. It cannot be written until you
   have a publisher ID — but it should be on the list the moment you do.

2. **`maxUnitsPerPage: 2` is declared and never read.** A grep across `src/` finds it only
   in `monetization.ts` and its own type declaration. Nothing enforces it. Three placements
   are set `reserveSpace: true`, so activating them would put three units on a calculator
   page while the config claims a cap of two.

3. **No CMP.** `ConsentState` defaults to all-denied and placements declare
   `requiresConsent: 'advertising'`, so the gating logic exists — but no consent vendor is
   wired in. EEA traffic requires a Google-certified CMP before AdSense will serve.

One thing to fix before activation, not after: **`reserveSpace` is a boolean with no
dimension.** Three placements reserve space, but no height is declared anywhere, so
there is nothing to reserve. That is exactly where CLS will come from on the day you
switch it on. Declare a per-placement height alongside the flag.
